# If Face ID isn't working on your iPhone or iPad Pro

If you need help using Face ID to unlock your iPhone or iPad, authenticate purchases, sign in to apps, or if you're asked to enter your passcode, learn what to do.

## Check for updates

[Update your iPhone or iPad to the latest version of iOS or iPadOS](https://support.apple.com/118575) . And make sure that [your iPhone or iPad model supports Face ID](https://support.apple.com/102854) .

## Check your Face ID settings

To check your Face ID settings, go to Settings, then tap Face ID & Passcode. Make sure that [Face ID is set up](https://support.apple.com/108411) and that the features you're trying to use Face ID with are turned on.

[Image: iPhone screen showing Face ID & Passcode settings with toggles for Face ID, iPhone Unlock, and other features.]

## Check that nothing is covering the TrueDepth camera

Remove anything that might be covering the TrueDepth camera like a smudge, case, or screen protector. On iPhone 18 Pro models, the TrueDepth camera is at the top of the device towards the left corner.

If you're using iPad or iPhone 18 Pro models in landscape orientation, make sure that your finger or palm isn't covering the TrueDepth camera.

[Image: When you use Face ID with your iPhone in portrait mode, you are less likely to cover the TrueDepth camera with your finger.]

## Make sure that your face isn't blocked

Make sure that your eyes, nose, and mouth are fully visible to the TrueDepth camera. By default, Face ID doesn't work if anything is covering your mouth and nose.

If you're wearing a face mask and have already [set up Face ID with a mask](https://support.apple.com/102452) ,* make sure that your eyes aren't blocked. You might need to hold your iPhone higher or to try moving your iPhone higher and closer if you're wearing a face mask. If you wear a face mask too high or too close to your eyes, your iPhone might not unlock.

Face ID works with many sunglasses, though Face ID with a mask isn't designed to work with sunglasses. If you're wearing sunglasses designed to block certain types of light, then your sunglasses might be blocking the infrared light used by the TrueDepth camera. Try using Face ID without your sunglasses.

If you can't get Face ID to work with the new TrueDepth camera on iPhone 18 Pro models , try :

- Removing privacy screen protectors

- Removing your glasses or adjusting your mask if you are wearing both in low light

- Removing heavy makeup or beauty masks

- Avoiding extreme backlight from the sun

## Add an alternate appearance

You might have to enter your passcode more often after you first set up Face ID. But for most users, Face ID will continuously learn as they change.

Face ID works with many prescription or clear glasses. If you can't get Face ID to work with clear or prescription glasses on iPhone 18 Pro models, try to [add an alternate appearance](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-up-face-id-iph6d162927a/ios) while wearing your glasses.

If you have an appearance that can look vastly different from facial hair, heavy makeup, or other changes, you can [set up an alternate appearance](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-up-face-id-iph6d162927a/ios) so Face ID still recognizes you.

## Make sure that you're facing the TrueDepth camera

Face ID works in portrait and landscape orientation on iPhone 13 models and later using iOS 16 and later. For earlier models, Face ID works on iPhone only when it's in portrait orientation and you're facing the TrueDepth camera. On iPad, you can use Face ID when your iPad is in any orientation.

When you use Face ID with a mask on your iPhone, it only works in portrait orientation. Face ID with a mask does not work on iPad.

The TrueDepth camera has a similar range of view as when you take a selfie or make a FaceTime call. Whether your iPhone or iPad is lying on a surface or you're holding it in a natural position, make sure that your iPhone or iPad is an arm's length or closer (10-20 inches) from your face.

## Restart your device

[Restart your iPhone](https://support.apple.com/118259) or [iPad](https://support.apple.com/101603) and enter your passcode. Then try using Face ID again.

## Reset Face ID

If you still need help, reset Face ID, then set it up again. Go to Settings, tap Face ID & Passcode, then tap Reset Face ID. Next tap [Set up Face ID](https://support.apple.com/108411) to set it up again.

## Get service

If Face ID repeatedly asks you to position your face within the frame without continuing the set up process or if your camera isn't working and you can't set up Face ID on your iPhone, you might need service. [Get service](https://getsupport.apple.com/?caller=kbase)

Only a trained technician should service your device. Improper repair, modification, or use of nongenuine Apple components in the laser systems might prevent the safety mechanisms from functioning properly, and could cause hazardous exposure and injury to eyes or skin.

[Learn when you must enter a passcode even if Face ID works as expected](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/face-id-touch-id-passcodes-and-passwords-sec9479035f1/web)

[Learn what to do if an alert says Face ID has been disabled](https://support.apple.com/105130)

* In portrait orientation, you can use Face ID with a mask on iPhone 12 and later using iOS 15.4 and later. Face ID with a mask doesn't work in landscape orientation.
