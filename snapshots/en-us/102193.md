# Using Apple extension cables and duckheads with Apple power adapters

Learn how to connect an Apple power adapter extension cable or AC plug (duckhead) to your Apple power adapter, and what to do if you have a damaged part.

## Connect an Apple extension cable or duckhead

On certain Apple power adapters, the AC plug (also known as a duckhead) is removable. You can replace it with an Apple extension cable to place the power adapter farther from the outlet, or replace it with an adapter for another country or region to charge your device while traveling. Just slide the duckhead off, then attach the extension cable or adapter.

[Image: Removal of a duckhead from an Apple power adapter. With the plug facing left, the duckhead is separated from the power adapter by pulling them apart vertically.]

The design of your power adapter's AC connector can differ depending on when the power adapter was made. Because of updated regulations, new extension cables and power adapters use AC Connector 2, which has a pill-shaped design. AC Connector 1 has a notched design. Extension cables and duckheads aren't cross-compatible between AC connector types, so make sure that your accessory has the correct AC connector for your power adapter.

## Tips to keep your extension cables and power adapters working properly

All Apple products — including power adapters and extension cables — undergo rigorous testing for safety and reliability, and are designed to meet government safety standards around the world. When used as designed, they safely provide power and recharge the batteries in your Apple products.

These tips can help you keep your extension cables and power adapters working properly:

- Never charge your device when moisture is present, as this can cause fire, electric shock, injury, or damage.

- Make sure you've fully inserted the extension cable or duckhead into your power adapter before connecting to a power outlet.

- When you unplug a cable from your product, a power source, or an AC adapter, hold on to the connector at the end of the cable. Pulling on the cable itself can strain the connector joints or cause breakage.

- If your AC adapter has blades or prongs that fold, fold them down when you store the adapter.

- Keep ports and connectors on your AC wall adapter and AC power cable free from foreign objects and debris.

- Make sure your adapter is used in a well-ventilated area. If used in a poorly ventilated space like under a blanket or on thick carpeting, the adapter might turn off to prevent overheating.

If your power adapter isn't working as expected, try these troubleshooting steps:

- Learn what to do [if your USB-C power adapter isn't charging your Mac laptop](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102464) .

- Learn what to do [if your MagSafe cable or power adapter isn't working](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102372) .

- If you're not sure which power adapter came with your Mac, [learn how to identify your Mac power adapter](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109509) .

## Identify damaged extension cables or AC power adapters

Signs of damage include but aren’t limited to:

- Any breakage or visible wear on a cable’s outer layer

- Bent, missing, loose, or broken AC prongs or blades

- AC blades that won’t lock into place, or that have black marks or other visible damage

- Sparks issuing from somewhere other than the blades of a plug

- Breakage, visible damage, or discoloration of the adapter casing

- Popping sounds from within the AC wall adapter or anywhere on the AC power cable

- AC adapters that don’t power your device

If the blade or prong on a power adapter or AC power cable is visibly loose, bent, or broken off and stuck inside an electrical outlet, there’s a safety risk. Follow these steps:

- If a blade or prong on an AC wall adapter or AC power cable seems damaged or gets stuck in a wall outlet, don’t repair, touch, or remove it, and don’t touch or use the electrical outlet.

- Remove the risk of electric shock from the affected outlet by turning off all power to that outlet from your circuit breaker or fuse box. If you’re unable to turn off the power to the electrical outlet, or can't confirm the power has been turned off, contact a qualified electrician.
