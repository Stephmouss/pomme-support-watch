# How to start up from macOS Recovery

macOS Recovery is your computer’s built-in recovery system, with utilities to reinstall macOS, repair or erase your startup disk, restore from a Time Machine backup, and more. Follow the steps for your type of Mac.

- [Mac with Apple silicon](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102518#applesilicon)

- [Intel-based Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102518#othermac)

## Mac with Apple silicon

Determine whether you’re using a [Mac with Apple silicon](https://support.apple.com/en-us/116943) . If you’re not sure, you can safely try the [steps for an Intel-based Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102518#othermac) as well.

[Image: Play the video]

### Start up from macOS Recovery (Mac with Apple silicon)

- Make sure that your Mac is turned off. If you can't shut it down normally, press and hold its power button for up to 10 seconds, until your Mac turns off. (Every Mac has a power button. On laptop computers that have Touch ID, press and hold the Touch ID button.)

- Press and hold the power button again. As you continue to hold it, your Mac turns on and loads [startup options](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102342) . When you see “Loading startup options” or the Options icon, release the power button.

- Click Options, then click the Continue button that appears beneath it.

[Image: Startup options screen on a Mac with Apple silicon.]

- If asked to select a volume to recover, select your startup disk, such as Macintosh HD. Then click Next.

- If asked to select a user you know the password for, select the user, click Next, then enter the password they use to log in to this Mac. [Forgot the password?](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102633)

- When your Mac has successfully started from Recovery, it shows a window with utilities such as these:

- [Restore from Time Machine](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102551)

- [Reinstall macOS](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102655)

- Disk Utility, which you can use to [repair](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102611) or [erase](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102506) your startup disk.

- Other utilities, such as Terminal, are available from the menu bar at the top of the screen.

[Image: Utilities window in macOS Recovery.]

- To quit Recovery, choose Restart or Shut Down from the Apple menu .

### If you can’t start up from macOS Recovery (Mac with Apple silicon)

If your Mac starts up to some other screen, such as a blank screen or exclamation point in a circle, learn what to do [if your Mac doesn't start up all the way](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102675) .

## Intel-based Mac

If you’re not using a Mac with Apple silicon, you’re using an Intel-based Mac. If you’re not sure, you can safely try the [steps for a Mac with Apple silicon](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102518#applesilicon) as well.

[Image: Play the video]

### Start up from macOS Recovery (Intel-based Mac)

- Make sure that your Mac is turned off. If you can't shut it down normally, press and hold its power button for up to 10 seconds, until your Mac turns off. (Every Mac has a power button. On laptop computers that have Touch ID, press and hold the Touch ID button.)

- Press and release the power button to turn on your Mac, then immediately press and hold these two keys on your keyboard: Command (⌘) and R. Keep holding them until you see an Apple logo or spinning globe.

- If asked to select a network, choose one from the Wi-Fi menu or attach a network cable.

[Image: Wi-Fi menu]

- If asked to select a volume to recover, select your startup disk, such as Macintosh HD. Then click Next.

- If asked to select a user you know the password for, select the user, click Next, then enter the password they use to log in to this Mac. [Forgot the password?](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102633)

- When your Mac has successfully started from Recovery, it shows a window with utilities such as these:

- [Restore from Time Machine](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102551)

- [Reinstall macOS](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102655)

- Disk Utility, which you can use to [repair](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102611) or [erase](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102506) your startup disk.

- Other utilities, such as Terminal, are available from the menu bar at the top of the screen.

[Image: Utilities window in macOS Recovery.]

- To quit Recovery, choose Restart or Shut Down from the Apple menu .

### If you can’t start up from macOS Recovery (Intel-based Mac)

To help ensure that your Mac can recognize the keys you’re pressing at startup:

- If you’re using a Mac laptop, use its built-in keyboard instead of an external keyboard.

- If you’re using an external keyboard, you might need to wait a few seconds before pressing any keys to give your Mac time to recognize the keyboard as it starts up. Some keyboards have a status light that flashes briefly when the keyboard is ready to use.

- If you’re using your keyboard wirelessly, plug it in to your Mac, if possible. Or try a wired keyboard.

- If your keyboard is made for PC, such as one that has a Windows logo, try a keyboard made for Mac.

If your Mac tries to start up from its built-in Recovery system but can’t, it should automatically show a spinning globe as it tries to start up from Recovery over the internet. Or you can force Internet Recovery by pressing either of the following combinations at startup, instead of Command-R. (Each combination also affects the version of macOS offered when [reinstalling macOS from Recovery](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102655) .)

- Option-Command-R

- Shift-Option-Command-R

If the spinning globe includes a warning symbol (exclamation point), startup from Internet Recovery was unsuccessful:

[Image: Globe with warning symbol when starting up from Internet Recovery.]

This warning screen and most numbered errors you might see here (such as -2001F, -2002F, or -2003F) are related to issues with your network or internet connection. Try these solutions:

- [Make sure that your Mac is connected to the internet](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101589) . It might also help to connect in a different way, such as via Ethernet instead of Wi-Fi.

- Try each key combination: Command-R, Option-Command-R, and Shift-Option-Command-R.

- Try when connected to a different network.

- Try again later, in case the issue is temporary.

- Learn what to do [if your Mac starts up to error -1008F](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102271) .

Other solutions:

- If your Mac starts up to some other screen, such as a blank screen, question mark, or lock symbol, learn what to do [if your Mac doesn't start up all the way](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102675) .

- If your Mac is currently set to start up from Microsoft Windows using Boot Camp, [set your Mac to start up from macOS](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102450) , then shut down and try again.
