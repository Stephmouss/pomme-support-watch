# Activation Lock for Mac

Activation Lock is designed to prevent others from using your Mac if it's lost or stolen.

## About Activation Lock

Activation Lock helps keep your Mac secure, even [if your Mac is lost or stolen](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102481) , and can improve your chances of recovering it. With Activation Lock, your Apple Account password or device passcode is required before anyone can turn off Find My, erase your Mac, or reactivate and use your Mac. Even if you erase your Mac remotely, Activation Lock can continue to deter others from reactivating your Mac without your permission. All you need to do is keep Find My turned on and remember your Apple Account email or phone number and password.

[Learn about Activation Lock for iPhone or iPad](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108794) .

## How to enable Activation Lock on Mac

If your Mac meets the [Activation Lock system requirements](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102541#requirements) , turn on [Find My](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102648) to enable Activation Lock. It remains enabled as long as you keep Find My turned on.

- From the Apple menu , choose System Settings.

- Click your name in the sidebar, then click iCloud on the right.

- Click the See All button (or Show More Apps button).

- Click Find My Mac, then click the button to turn it on.

## How to disable Activation Lock on Mac

To disable Activation Lock, turn off Find My:

- From the Apple menu , choose System Settings.

- Click your name in the sidebar, then click iCloud on the right.

- Click the See All button (or Show More Apps button).

- Click Find My Mac, then click the button to turn it off. Enter your Apple Account password when asked.

Or if the Mac is offline, you can disable Activation Lock on the web:

- Go to [www.iCloud.com/find](https://www.icloud.com/find) .

- Sign in to your Apple Account.

- Select the Mac that you want to remove from iCloud.

- Click Remove.

When a part from one product is used to repair another product, that part could be locked to someone else’s Apple Account. If your [Mac with Apple silicon](https://support.apple.com/en-us/116943) and [macOS Tahoe 26 or later](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109033) was previously repaired, or you’re not sure, [use Repair Assistant to check for an unfinished repair](https://support.apple.com/en-us/123128) .

## System requirements for Activation Lock

[Mac with Apple silicon](https://support.apple.com/en-us/116943) and:

- macOS Catalina 10.15 or later

- [Security policy set to Full Security](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/change-startup-disk-security-settings-a-mac-mchl768f7291/mac) , which is the default setting

- [Two-factor authentication for Apple Account](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102660)

[Mac with the Apple T2 Security Chip](https://support.apple.com/en-us/103265) and:

- macOS Catalina 10.15 or later

- [Startup security set to Secure Boot and “Disallow booting from external or removable media"](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102522) , which are the default settings

- [Two-factor authentication for Apple Account](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102660)
