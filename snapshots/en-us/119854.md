# Family Sharing and parental controls overview for kids and teens

iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, and Mac are some of the most powerful tools ever made for learning, exploring, and staying in touch. Family Sharing, Screen Time, and other parental controls help you have a fun and safe experience while taking advantage of everything that your Apple device has to offer.

Age requirements for Apple Account and Family Sharing [vary by state, country or region](https://support.apple.com/en-us/125666) .

## Join a Family Sharing group

When you join a Family Sharing group, you can share subscriptions and more with your family. Family Sharing also helps your parent or guardian make sure that you have a fun and safe experience on your Apple device. You might start with a focused set of apps and websites available on your device, with more added as you grow.

If you’re under 13:

- A parent or guardian creates an Apple Account for you, and it's added to your Family Sharing group.

If you’re between 13 and 17:

- In some states, countries and regions, you can create an Apple Account yourself or have a parent or guardian create an account for you.

- Depending on the laws where you live, your parent or guardian might have to create your Apple Account. You might also have to join a Family Sharing group.

## Learn about the settings for your Family Sharing group

It’s easy to see details about your Family Sharing group and which parental controls are set for your account.

Just open the Settings app on your iPhone or iPad (or System Settings on Mac), select Family, then select your name.

Here's some of what you'll see:

- Personal Information: Your account details, location settings, age range, and the parents or guardians who can manage parental controls for your account.

- Screen Time settings and parental controls: The Screen Time settings and parental controls that your parent or guardian has turned on — like approved Apps & Websites, Screen Time Schedule, Time Allowances, Allowed Contacts, and Content & Privacy Restrictions.

[Image: Family Sharing overview for a family member.]

[Depending on the laws where you live](https://support.apple.com/en-us/125666) , certain settings are automatically turned on based on your age. In some places, settings like Ask to Buy and content restrictions for apps can't be turned off.

### Stay informed about your Family Sharing group

- When something changes, you’ll get a notification — like when a new parent or guardian joins your group or when your parent or guardian changes your Share My Location settings.

- These notifications appear on your Home Screen or Lock Screen (just like text messages), and you'll see a red exclamation mark in the Settings app.

## Ask for approval

Sometimes, you might want to do something that your parent or guardian needs to approve — like texting a new friend or downloading an app. When that happens, just ask. Your parent or guardian can receive your request in Messages and can approve it there or on your device.

- Ask to Buy: When you want to download or buy certain things (like an eligible app, TV show, movie, or book), you get the option to tap Ask or "approve in person" if your parent or guardian is nearby. Once they approve, the item downloads automatically.

- Ask to Browse: If you want to visit a website in Safari or a supported browser app that your parent or guardian hasn't approved yet, you can send them a request. They can review it in Messages and approve it. Once they do, the website becomes visible.

- Ask for an exception: If the app you want has an age rating that's higher than what you're allowed to access, you can ask your parent or guardian for an exception to download it. If you can't find an app that you used to have, the app's age rating might have changed. The app may still be available in the App Store if you search for it. You can ask your parent or guardian for an exception to download it again.

- Ask to communicate: If you want to communicate with someone through Phone, FaceTime, Messages, or another supported app who isn't on your approved contacts list, you can ask your parent or guardian to add them.

- Ask for more time: If you've hit your Time Allowance, you can tap One More Minute or send a request for more time.

[Learn what your parent or guardian can do if they don't receive your request](https://support.apple.com/125399#requests)

## How parental controls work

With Family Sharing and Screen Time, your parent or guardian can help make sure that you have a safe experience when you're using your Apple device.

### Content and screen time

Your parent can help you choose age-appropriate content and manage how much time you spend on apps and websites.

- App & Website Activity — Your parent or guardian can see which apps and websites that you use most, and help you decide when and how long to use them. They can set a Screen Time Schedule to manage when you use apps during the day, like during school, and set Time Allowances for how long you spend in categories like entertainment, games, and social media.

- Content & Privacy Restrictions — Your parent or guardian can manage content, apps, and settings on your device, including limiting access to certain apps, games, websites, and other content, and prevent changes to important account settings.

[Image: Content & Privacy Restrictions screen.]

- Age Range for Apps — Your parent or guardian can allow you to share your age range with apps to help provide an age-appropriate experience, while keeping your birth date private. Depending on the laws where you live, Age Range for Apps might be automatically turned on based on your age and can't be turned off.

- Limit Adult Websites — This setting blocks detected adult content on the web to help make sure that you see content that is right for your age. If you're under 18, this feature is turned on automatically for your new account or when you update to the latest software version. Depending on the laws where you live, if you want to turn this setting off, you might need an adult in your Family Sharing group who has confirmed their age to do so.

[Image: Apps and Websites screen showing website restrictions and a list of allowed apps.]

### Talk and text safely

Your parent or guardian can choose who you can talk or text with and when — but they can't read your messages or listen to your conversations.

- Allowed Contacts — Your parent or guardian can choose who you can communicate with through Phone, FaceTime, Messages, and other supported apps, and where and when you can communicate with them.

- Communication Safety — This feature helps when you receive or try to send a photo or video with detected nudity or gore and violence. Communication Safety gives you an extra moment to pause and think — and, if you need more help, you can find resources to get the help that you need. If you're under 18, Communication Safety is turned on automatically for your new account or when you update to the latest software version. Depending on the laws where you live, if you want to turn this setting off, you might need an adult in your Family Sharing group who has confirmed their age to do so.

[Image: Communication Safety settings for a family member.]

### Privacy and safety

Your location and personal information are sensitive. Apple gives you control over what you share.

#### Location sharing

- When your family organizer sets up location sharing, you might be able to choose whether or not to share your location with the rest of the group.

- Apps have to ask to use your location. The first time an app wants or needs to use your location, you choose if and when to share that information. When an app is using your location from Location Services, an arrow icon appears in the status bar at the top of your iPhone or in Control Center.

- Your parent or guardian can prevent changes to certain privacy settings, including Share My Location settings.

[Image: Location Sharing settings with a list of family members who can see the location of the pictured device.]

#### Personal information

- When you see the Data and Privacy icon , Apple is asking to use your personal information.

[Image: Data and Privacy icon in the Music app.]

- When Apple uses your personal information, you have the chance to read more about what data is collected and how it’s used. If you need help, ask your parent or guardian to read and explain the information to you.

- At any time, you can manage your data, see how it’s stored, and ask for a copy of that data — just visit your [Data & Privacy page](https://privacy.apple.com/account) .

- If you’re under 13, the family organizer needs to give permission when you use the Data & Privacy page to make a request related to your data.

### Use intelligence features safely

Your Apple device has intelligence features that help you be more creative and get things done faster — and they're designed to protect your privacy at every step. With Content & Privacy Restrictions, your parent or guardian can choose to allow or not allow these features:

- Siri — If you're 13 or older, Siri AI is available if your parent or guardian allows. They can also help decide to allow Broad World Knowledge, which determines whether Siri can use web content to answer world information and general knowledge questions. If you're younger than 13, your parent or guardian can allow access to a previous version of Siri.

- Writing Tools — Use Apple Intelligence to proofread, rewrite, and summarize text in most places you write, like in Mail or Notes.

- Apple Intelligence image creation tools — Image Playground, Genmoji, and Image Wand help you create original images and custom emojis, just by describing what you want.

- Intelligence Extensions — If you're 13 or older, your parent or guardian can allow you to access Intelligence Extensions from Siri, Writing Tools, and more. If you use ChatGPT, you must be at least 13 years old or the minimum age required in your country to consent to use ChatGPT. When using the ChatGPT extension, you choose what content to send to ChatGPT. [Learn more about using ChatGPT with Apple Intelligence](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/iph00fd3c8c2/ios) .

- Math Notes — Solve math problems, use variables, and even graph equations right in the Notes app.

## When you’re ready to manage your own device

Age requirements for Apple Account and Family Sharing [vary by state, country, or region](https://support.apple.com/en-us/125666) .

Depending on your age and where you live, you might be able to [leave your Family Sharing group](https://support.apple.com/102652) .

- Under 13 — You need to be part of a Family Sharing group.

- Ages 13-17 — If your parent or guardian has set up Screen Time, you need permission from your family organizer to leave the group. If Screen Time isn't turned on, talk to your parent or guardian about the benefits of staying in the Family Sharing group. Depending on the laws where you live, you might not be able to leave your Family Sharing group until you turn 18.

- When you turn 18, you can decide to leave your Family Sharing group or manage your own Screen Time settings. In some places, you might need to confirm that you're 18 or older before you can change certain settings or leave your Family Sharing group.
