# See your Apple Card recurring transactions

In the Wallet app, you can see upcoming transactions, review what you've been charged before, and keep track of your recurring spending on your Apple Card.

## Learn about recurring transactions

Recurring transactions are charges that Apple Card identifies as happening on a regular schedule, such as a monthly subscription, an annual membership, or a utility bill. Apple Card uses your transaction history with each merchant to find recurring charges, then estimates your next charge amount and date.

## See your recurring transactions

- On your iPhone, open the Wallet app and tap Apple Card.

- Tap the More button , then tap Recurring.

- Choose an option:

- Tap Upcoming to see expected transactions on a calendar, then scroll down to see upcoming expected transactions and recent transactions.

- Tap All to see every transaction identified as recurring.

In the Upcoming view, expected transactions appear on a monthly calendar, with the total expected amount shown under each date. In the All view, you can see each recurring transaction and how often the charge repeats such as Monthly or Yearly, and the next expected date.

[Image: Recurring transactions in Apple Card showing a calendar and upcoming transactions.]

## See details for a recurring transaction

- In the Recurring view, tap a merchant to see:

- Expected amount

- Expected date

- How often the charge repeats

- Last four digits of the card number used

- Scroll down to Recent Transactions to see recent charges from the merchant or tap See All to see all previous transactions.

[Image: Transaction details in Apple Card showing the expected charge and previous transactions.]

## Mark a transaction as not recurring

If a transaction appears in Recurring but isn't a recurring transaction, you can remove it from your recurring transactions.

- In the Recurring view, tap the merchant.

- Scroll to the bottom, then tap Mark as Not Recurring.

[Image: Mark as Not Recurring button in transaction details.]

## See how recurring transactions are displayed

Apple Card predicts upcoming dates and amounts from your past transactions with each merchant. They're estimates, not confirmed charges, so they may not be exact and a predicted charge may not happen at all.

- Recent cancellations and newly added subscriptions may not be reflected right away. If you cancel a subscription or service, a transaction may keep appearing as recurring until an expected charge doesn't appear from the merchant.

- If a subscription price or merchant charge increases, the amount updates once the transaction appears, so future predictions reflect the new price going forward.

- Refunds and disputes don't change your past transactions or upcoming predictions, since predictions are based on your recurring pattern with a merchant, not any single charge. A refund appears as its own separate transaction and counts toward your spending trends and totals.

- Amounts shown in the calendar view are rounded. For example, $39.42 appears as $39 and $4,567 as $4.6k. To see the actual amount, tap the date on the calendar.

- A "Variable" badge appears on transactions like a utility bill, where the amount changes from cycle to cycle even though the charge arrives on the same schedule.

- Apple Card Family Owners and Co-Owners see Recurring transactions across the whole account, including participants. Participants can see only their transactions.

- Apple Card Monthly Installments* don't appear in the Recurring view.

- Subscriptions billed together by Apple, such as Apple One, may appear as a single recurring transaction labeled "Apple" rather than as separate charges for each service in the bundle.

- Everyday spending may sometimes appear in Recurring even when it isn't. To remove a transaction from Recurring, you can [Mark as Not Recurring](https://support.apple.com/en-us/127699#mark) .
