# What to do if you get an alert that an AirTag, set of AirPods, Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device is with you

Learn how to receive unwanted tracking alerts on your iPhone or iPad and what to do if an AirTag, AirPods, Find My network accessory, or another compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device may be moving with you.

AirTag, devices that are part of the Find My network accessory program, and some AirPods models — AirPods 3, AirPods 4 with Active Noise Cancellation (ANC), AirPods Pro, and AirPods Max — use the Find My network to help you keep track of and find your belongings.* After you set up a compatible item, it shows up in the Devices or Items tab in the Find My app.

AirTag, AirPods, and other Find My network accessories include features to guard against unwanted tracking. They should not be used to track people, and should not be used to track property that does not belong to you. Using these products to track people without their consent is a crime in many countries and regions around the world. If an AirTag, set of AirPods, or Find My network accessory is discovered to be unlawfully tracking a person, [law enforcement can request any available information](https://www.apple.com/legal/transparency/government-information.html) from Apple to support their investigation.

AirTag, AirPods, and Find My network accessories are designed to protect your privacy when you're using them to keep track of important items. They have unique Bluetooth identifiers that change frequently. The Find My network uses end-to-end encryption and is [built with privacy in mind](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/find-my/) .

To further combat the misuse of Bluetooth location-tracking devices, Apple and Google worked together to create an industry specification that makes it possible to alert users — across both iOS and Android — if such a device is unknowingly being used to track them. This helps mitigate the misuse of devices designed to help keep track of belongings.

## How to receive unwanted tracking alerts

If any AirTag, AirPods, Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device is separated from its owner and seen moving with you over time, you're notified by a notification on your device or a sound emitted by the device. These features were created specifically to discourage people from trying to track you without your knowledge.

To receive Tracking Notifications on your Apple device, make sure that you:

- Update your iPhone or iPad to iOS 17.5 or later or iPadOS 17.5 or later.*

- Go to Settings, tap Privacy & Security, and select Location Services. Then:

- Turn Location Services on.

- Scroll down and tap System Services, then turn Significant Locations on to be notified when you arrive at a significant location, such as your home.

- Go to Settings, then tap Bluetooth. Turn on Bluetooth.

- Go to Settings, tap Notifications, then tap Tracking Notifications. Turn on Allow Notifications.

- Turn off Airplane Mode. If your device is in Airplane Mode, you won't receive tracking notifications.

* The minimum system requirements for Tracking Notifications are iOS 14.5 or later or iPadOS 14.5 or later. Compatible Bluetooth location-tracking devices require iOS 17.5 or later and iPadOS 17.5 or later.

## If you see an alert or hear a sound

If you see one of the following alerts on your iPhone or iPad, a Find My network item or a compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device could be traveling with you, and the owner might be able to see its location.

It's possible that the AirTag or Find My network accessory might be attached to an item you're borrowing. You might also get this alert if you’re borrowing someone else’s AirPods. If you're borrowing an AirTag or another item compatible with the Find My network from a member of your Family Sharing group, you can tap the notification and choose to turn off alerts for that item for one day, or indefinitely.

### AirTag Found Moving With You

If an AirTag that's separated from its owner is seen moving with you over time, or if your iPhone is awake and an AirTag that isn't with its owner for a period of time emits a sound to indicate that it has moved, this alert is displayed: AirTag Found Moving With You.

[Image: iPhone showing AirTag Found Moving With You notification at the bottom of the screen.]

Find My displays a map of where the AirTag, set of AirPods, Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device has been observed with you. The red dots show where the unknown item was detected near your iPhone or iPad. The dashed lines connecting the red dots help indicate the sequence where the item was detected with you. The red dots do not indicate when the item's owner is viewing the item's location.

### AirPods Detected, AirPods Pro Detected, or AirPods Max Detected

If AirPods that are separated from their owner are seen moving with you over time, one of these alerts is displayed: AirPods Detected, AirPods Pro Detected, or AirPods Max Detected.

[Image: iPhone showing AirPod Found Moving With You notification at the bottom of the screen.]

### "Product Name" Found Moving With You

If a Find My network accessory or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device that's separated from its owner is seen moving with you over time, this alert is displayed: "Product Name" Found Moving With You (where "Product Name" is the name of the Find My network accessory or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device that has been detected).

### Unknown Accessory Detected

If a set of AirPods, a Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device is detected but the Find My app has issues connecting to them or to the server at that time, this alert is displayed: Unknown Accessory Detected. This alert displays only if a set of AirPods, a Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device is detected near you, not an AirTag. These could be borrowed AirPods, or AirPods inadvertently left behind by a friend or family member.

### If you hear an AirTag, Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device emitting a sound

To alert people nearby, an AirTag, AirPods Pro 2 charging case, AirPods Pro 3 charging case, Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device separated for a period of time from its owner emits a sound when it's moved.

## Find the unknown AirTag, set of AirPods, Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device

If you get an alert that an AirTag, set of AirPods, Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device is traveling with you, follow these steps to find it.

### Play a sound using the Find My app

- Tap the alert.

- Tap Continue and then tap Play Sound.

- Listen for the sound. You can play it again if you need more time to find the item.

If the option to play a sound isn't available, the item might not be with you anymore or it's within range of its owner. Or if it was with you overnight, its identifier might have changed. Find My uses the identifier to determine that it's the same item moving with you.

### Locate it using Find Nearby

If the unknown accessory is an AirTag or AirPods Pro 3 charging case and you have a [supported iPhone model with Ultra Wideband](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109512) , you can tap Find Nearby to use Precision Finding to help you locate the unknown AirTag.

- Tap the alert.

- Tap Continue, then tap Find Nearby.

- Follow the onscreen instructions and move around the space until your iPhone connects to the unknown AirTag.

- Your iPhone displays the distance and direction to the unknown AirTag. Use that information to get closer to the unknown AirTag until you find it.

- When the AirTag is within Bluetooth range of your iPhone, you can play a sound on the AirTag by tapping the Play Sound button .

[Image: No alt supplied for Image]

- If your iPhone says that more light is required, tap the Turn Flashlight On button .

[Image: No alt supplied for Image]

- When you're done, tap the Done button .

[Image: No alt supplied for Image]

### Look through your belongings

If you can't play a sound, hear a sound, or locate the item with Precision Finding and you believe the item is still with you, look through your belongings to try to find it. Check on your person or in anything in your possession that could contain this device. It could be in an item that you don’t check often, like a jacket pocket, the outer compartment of a bag, or your vehicle. If you can't find the device and if you feel your safety is at risk, go to a safe public location and contact law enforcement.

## Get information about or disable an AirTag, set of AirPods, Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device

If you find an unknown AirTag, you can get information about it or to disable it.

### Get information about an AirTag

- If you have found an AirTag, hold the top of your iPhone or NFC-capable smartphone near the white side of the AirTag until a notification appears.

- Tap the notification. This opens a website that provides information about the AirTag, including its serial number and the last four digits of the phone number of the person who registered it. This can help you identify the owner, if you know them. You might want to [take a screenshot](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102616) to make sure that you can document the information.

[Image: iPhone screen showing a Safari webpage with information about your AirTag, including the serial number and last four digits of the owner’s phone number.]

- If the owner marked the AirTag as lost, you might see a message with information about how to contact the owner.

To learn more about a set of AirPods, AirTag, Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device that’s been separated from its owner, scroll to the bottom of the Items tab, then tap Identify Found Item.

### See a list of your Unwanted Tracking notifications

To see a list of all AirTags, Find My Network accessories, and compatible Bluetooth location-tracking devices about which you have been notified:

- Open the Find My app on your iPhone or iPad.

- Tap Items.

- To see items that recently triggered an unwanted tracking alert, scroll to the bottom and tap Unknown Items Detected with You. If the option to play a sound isn't available, the item might not be with you anymore, might be near its owner, or if it was with you overnight, its identifier might have changed.

[Image: If an unknown item is detected with you, you can view it on a map and learn how long it’s been detected with you in the Find My app.]

### Disable an AirTag, set of AirPods, Find My Network accessory, or compatible location-tracking device

To disable the AirTag, set of AirPods, Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-sharing device and stop it from sharing its location, tap Instructions to Disable and follow the onscreen steps. After you disable the AirTag, set of AirPods, Find My network accessory, or compatible Bluetooth location-tracking device, the owner can no longer get updates on its current location. You will also no longer receive any unwanted tracking alerts for this item.

[Image: iPhone screen showing a Safari webpage with instructions for how to disable an AirTag by removing its battery.]

For compatible Bluetooth location-tracking devices, disable instructions and additional information come from their respective finder network and have not been certified by Apple. Product images, instructions, and descriptions may not be available, or may not be available in all languages.

If you feel your safety is at risk, contact your local law enforcement, who can [work with Apple to request information related to the item](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/law-enforcement-guidelines-us.pdf) . You might need to provide the AirTag, set of AirPods, or Find My network accessory, and the device's serial number.

Disabling the Find My network on your device, turning off Bluetooth, or turning off Location Services on your iPhone does not prevent the owner of the AirTag, AirPods, or Find My network accessory from being able to see the location of that AirTag or accessory. You must disable the item by following the instructions above.

## Check for nearby AirTag or Find My network accessories with an Android device

Devices with Android 6.0 or later can automatically notify you if an AirTag or another item tracker is moving with you. On your Android device, you can also run a manual scan to check if an unknown item is moving with you at any time.

[Learn more about how to find unknown trackers on Android](https://support.google.com/android/answer/13658562)

Or you can download the Tracker Detect app from the Google Play Store. Tracker Detect looks for item trackers within Bluetooth range that are separated from their owner and that are compatible with Apple's Find My network. These include AirTag and compatible item trackers that use the Find My network. If you think someone is using an AirTag or another item tracker to track your location, you can scan to try to find it. If the app detects an AirTag or compatible item tracker near you for at least 10 minutes, you can play a sound to help find it.

[Go to Google Play to download the Tracker Detect app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.apple.trackerdetect)
