# About Wi-Fi Assist

When you have a poor Wi-Fi connection, you can use Wi-Fi Assist to automatically switch to cellular.

## How Wi-Fi Assist works

With Wi-Fi Assist, you can stay connected to the Internet even if you have a poor Wi-Fi connection. For example, if you're using Safari with a poor Wi-Fi connection and a webpage doesn't load, Wi-Fi Assist will activate and automatically switch to cellular. This allows the webpage to continue loading. You can use Wi-Fi Assist with most apps like Safari, Apple Music, Mail, Maps, and more.

When you activate Wi-Fi Assist, you'll see the cellular data icon in the status bar on your device.

Because you'll stay connected to the Internet over cellular when you have a poor Wi-Fi connection, you might use more cellular data. For most users, this should only be a small percentage higher than previous usage. If you have questions about your data usage, learn more about [managing your cellular data](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109323) .

You can use Wi-Fi Assist with any iOS device with iOS 9 or later, except for these models: iPhone 4s, iPad 2 Wi-Fi+Cellular, iPad (3rd generation) Wi-Fi+Cellular, and iPad mini (1st generation) Wi-Fi+Cellular.

## Turn Wi-Fi Assist on or off

Wi-Fi Assist is on by default. If you don't want your iOS device to stay connected to the Internet when you have a poor Wi-Fi connection, you can disable Wi-Fi Assist.

- Open the Settings app.

- Tap Cellular or tap Mobile Data.

- Scroll down and tap the slider for Wi-Fi Assist.

## Learn more

- If you're data roaming, Wi-Fi Assist won't automatically switch to cellular.

- Wi-Fi Assist only works when you have apps running in the foreground and doesn't activate with background downloading of content.

- Wi-Fi Assist doesn’t activate with some third-party apps that stream audio or video, or download attachments, like an email app, as these apps and attachments might use large amounts of data.
