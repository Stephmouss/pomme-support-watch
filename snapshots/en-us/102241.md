# Edit HDR video recorded on an iPhone or iPad

You can use Apple apps like Clips, iMovie, and Final Cut Pro for Mac to edit and share Dolby Vision High Dynamic Range (HDR) videos recorded with certain iPhone and iPad models.

Certain iPhone and iPad models can record video in Dolby Vision HDR. To check that your device is set to record HDR video, go to Settings > Camera > Record Video and make sure HDR Video is turned on.

To learn whether your device supports playing video in HDR, recording video in HDR, or both, identify your [iPhone model](https://support.apple.com/kb/HT201296) or [iPad model](https://support.apple.com/kb/HT201471) , then [check its specifications](https://support.apple.com/specs/iphone) .

[Learn more about playing and recording HDR video on your iPhone and iPad](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/iph2cafe2ebc/ios/)

## Edit HDR video with Clips on iPhone and iPad

With the Clips app, create a new project and record HDR video directly from within the app or import an HDR video clip recorded with your device.

[Learn how to use the Clips app](https://support.apple.com/guide/clips/welcome/ios)

## Edit HDR video with iMovie on iPhone or iPad

In iMovie for iOS and iPad OS, you can add HDR video recorded with your device to your iMovie project. When sharing your movie, tap Options, then turn HDR on to export your movie in HDR. To share a Standard Dynamic Range (SDR) version of your movie, turn HDR off.

[Learn how to use iMovie on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/imovie-iphone/welcome/ios)

[Learn how to use iMovie on iPad](https://support.apple.com/guide/imovie-ipad/welcome/ipados)

## Edit HDR video with iMovie on Mac

In iMovie version 10.2 or later on Mac, you can import HDR video into an iMovie project. For HDR video imported in an earlier version of iMovie, you'll need to reimport it so it displays properly. You can share from iMovie in SDR.

To share an iMovie for macOS project in HDR, you need to export your iMovie project to Final Cut Pro, then share using Final Cut Pro. When you send a movie to Final Cut Pro, the project file and the media used in the project are copied to your Final Cut Pro library. Make sure to install [Final Cut Pro](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/final-cut-pro/id424389933) or the [Final Cut Pro trial](https://www.apple.com/final-cut-pro/trial/) on your Mac. Then, in iMovie, choose File > Send Movie to Final Cut Pro. To share your project from Final Cut Pro, follow the steps in the Final Cut Pro section below.

[Learn more about sending iMovie projects to Final Cut Pro](https://support.apple.com/guide/imovie/send-projects-to-final-cut-pro-movcbf7e2a3f/mac)

## Edit HDR video with Final Cut Pro on Mac

- [Create a new Final Cut Pro library](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/vera0e150af2/mac) and set the color processing for the library to [Wide Gamut HDR](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/ver1cd9629a5/mac/) .

- Import HDR video recorded with iPhone. You can import HDR video [directly from your iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/ver41815001/mac) or from [the Photos app](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/verc5d33882/mac) .

- [Create a new project](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/verdb79783e/mac) and set the color space for your project to [Wide Gamut HDR - Rec. 2020 HLG](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/ver1cd9629a5/mac) .

- Edit your project.

- For the highest quality, [export an HLG master file](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/ver504d31dd2/mac) or [share to Apple devices](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/ver3675a4f8c/mac) with the video codec set to HEVC 10-bit.

### Share an HDR project from Final Cut Pro to YouTube

If you want to upload an HDR version of your project to YouTube, [export an HLG master file](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/ver504d31dd2/mac) or [Share to Apple devices](https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/ver3675a4f8c/mac) , then upload the video file to YouTube with a web browser like Safari.
