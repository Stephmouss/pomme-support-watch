# Use Made for iPhone hearing devices

Use Made for iPhone (MFi) hearing aids to listen and talk on your iPhone, iPad, Mac, or Apple Vision Pro.

- [Check compatibility with your Apple device](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#compatibility)

- [Put your hearing device in pairing mode](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#pairing)

- [Pair and connect your hearing device](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#connect)

- [Control your hearing device](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#control)

- [Unpair your hearing device](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#unpair)

- [If you need help](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#help)

## Check compatibility with your Apple device

Check the list of MFi hearing devices and system requirements to find out whether your hearing device is compatible with your Apple device and its operating system.

[List of Made for iPhone hearing devices](https://support.apple.com/kb/HT210386)

## Put your hearing device in pairing mode

If your hearing device is compatible, bring it near your Apple device, then put it in pairing mode to make it discoverable by your Apple device. The manual for your hearing device explains how, but these methods are typical:

- If your hearing device has batteries that you can replace, open the battery door on each device. Then close the door when you're ready to pair and connect to your Apple device.

- If your hearing device has an on/off switch, turn it off and back on when you're ready to pair and connect to your Apple device.

- If your hearing device has built-in rechargeable batteries and no on/off switch, remove the device from its charger when you're ready to pair and connect to your Apple device.

## Pair and connect your hearing device

After checking compatibility and putting your hearing device in pairing mode, follow these steps to pair and connect it to your Apple device. If you previously paired a different hearing device, your new hearing device replaces the previous one.

### iPhone or iPad

- Put your hearing device in [pairing mode](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#pairing) and bring it near your iPhone or iPad.

- Open the Settings app.

- Tap Accessibility, then tap Hearing Devices to begin searching for your hearing device.

[Image: Hearing Devices settings when searching for MFi hearing devices]

- When your hearing device appears in settings, tap its name.

- Tap Pair when you see one or more Bluetooth pairing requests.

### Mac

- Put your hearing device in [pairing mode](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#pairing) and bring it near your Mac.

- From the Apple menu  in the corner of your screen, choose System Settings.

- Click Accessibility in the sidebar, then click Hearing Devices on the right to begin searching for your hearing device. (Hearing Devices appears only if your Mac and macOS are [compatible with MFi hearing devices](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#compatibility) .)

[Image: Hearing Devices settings when searching for MFi hearing devices]

- When your hearing device appears in settings, click Connect.

### Apple Vision Pro

- Put your hearing device in [pairing mode](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#pairing) and bring it near your Apple Vision Pro.

- Open the Settings app.

- Tap Accessibility, then tap Hearing Devices.

- When your hearing device appears in settings, tap its name.

- Tap Pair when you see one or more Bluetooth pairing requests.

## Control your hearing device

When your hearing device is connected to it, your Apple device can control microphone level (volume), audio presets, and more for your hearing device. Find the settings here:

- iPhone, iPad, or Apple Vision Pro: Open Settings, tap Accessibility, then tap Hearing Devices. To see more settings, tap the name of your connected hearing device.

- Mac: From the Apple menu , choose System Settings. Click Accessibility in the sidebar, then click Hearing Devices on the right. [Learn more about Hearing Devices settings](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/change-hearing-devices-settings-accessibility-mchl096a5035/mac) .

For quicker access to some settings and features:

- Use the Hearing module in Control Center. To add the Hearing module, learn how to [customize Control Center on iPhone or iPad](https://support.apple.com/kb/HT202769) or [customize Control Center on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/quickly-change-settings-mchl50f94f8f/mac) .

- Use the [Accessibility Shortcut on iPhone or iPad](https://support.apple.com/kb/HT204390) or the [Accessibility Shortcuts panel on Mac](https://support.apple.com/kb/HT202562) .

On iPhone and iPad, you can also [turn on Live Listen](https://support.apple.com/kb/HT203990) from the settings screen.

If your hearing device has its own app from the manufacturer, that app might offer additional settings and controls.

## Unpair your hearing device

When you unpair your hearing device from your Apple device, it disconnects and will not reconnect to your Apple device until you pair it again.

- iPhone, iPad, Apple Vision Pro: Open Settings, tap Accessibility, then Hearing Devices. Tap the name of your hearing device, then scroll down and tap "Forget this device."

- Mac: From the Apple menu , choose System Settings. Click Accessibility in the sidebar, then click Hearing Devices on the right. Scroll down and tap "Forget this device."

## If you need help

Try these solutions if your hearing device doesn't connect or work as expected.

### Hearing device doesn't appear in Hearing Devices settings

- [Make sure that your hearing device is compatible with your Apple device](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#compatibility) .

- [Make sure that your hearing device is in pairing mode](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#pairing) .

- Restart your Apple device.

- If your hearing device is paired with a different Apple device, [unpair it](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#unpair) from that device. Then try again on your other Apple device.

- Make sure that your hearing device is updated with the latest firmware from the manufacturer. If it has its own app, that app can probably find and install firmware updates.

### Hearing device is dimmed in Hearing Devices settings

- Make sure that your hearing device is near your Apple device.

- [Unpair your hearing device](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108780#unpair) , if possible, then pair it again.

- On Mac, if you're using a Bluetooth mouse, trackpad, keyboard, or other Bluetooth input device, turn off the input device. If that allows your hearing device to connect, your input device is incompatible with the use of MFi hearing devices on Mac. Solutions:

- Use a wired input device, which connects to your Mac via USB instead of Bluetooth.

- Use a Bluetooth input device that is known to work with MFi hearing devices on Mac, such as the latest models (not earlier models) of [Apple Magic Keyboard, Magic Mouse, or Magic Trackpad](https://support.apple.com/kb/HT201178) .

### Hearing device disconnects unexpectedly

- When using your hearing device, make sure that your paired Apple device remains nearby.

- If your hearing device is paired with more than one Apple device, it might switch (hand off) to the other Apple device when it detects an incoming call on that device, or when you start playing audio on the other device. You can control this by changing the Audio Handoff setting in Hearing Devices settings on your iPhone or iPad.

- Check the battery level of your hearing device. You might need to replace or recharge the batteries.

- [Check for wireless interference](https://support.apple.com/kb/HT201542) .

### Hearing device is connected but you don't hear audio

- [Choose your hearing device as the sound output device](https://support.apple.com/kb/HT202809) .

- Check the microphone level in Hearing Devices settings.

### Mouse or trackpad feels different while using your hearing device

While using your hearing device with a Mac, the performance of a Bluetooth pointing device such a mouse or trackpad might feel slightly different when moving the pointer or clicking. This is expected and necessary to get the best performance from your MFi hearing device, which also uses Bluetooth. This doesn't affect the built-in trackpad of Mac laptop computers, or any pointing device that connects to your Mac via USB instead of Bluetooth.
