# If iCloud Mail isn't working

Learn what to do if you can't access iCloud Mail or you can't send and receive messages with your @icloud.com email address in the Mail app on your iPhone, iPad, Apple Vision Pro, or Mac, or on iCloud.com.

- [If you can't send or receive mail on your iPhone, iPad, or Apple Vision Pro](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102562#iphone)

- [If you can't send or receive mail from the Mail app on your Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102562#macOS)

- [If you can't access iCloud Mail at iCloud.com](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102562#web)

- [Get more help](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102562#help)

## If you can't send or receive mail on your iPhone, iPad, or Apple Vision Pro

Make sure that you turned on iCloud Mail and that your device and iCloud Mail account settings are up to date. Then make sure that data pushes to your device automatically.

### Turn on iCloud Mail on your iPhone, iPad, or Apple Vision Pro

- Open Settings, then tap your name.

- Tap iCloud, then tap Mail under Saved to iCloud or Apps Using iCloud.

- Turn on Use on this iPhone.

### Check your device and account settings

- Update to the [latest version of iOS](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118575) , [iPadOS](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118575) , or [visionOS](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118481) .

- Make sure that you're connected to the internet and [check the system status of iCloud Mail](https://www.apple.com/support/systemstatus/) . If iCloud Mail isn't available, try again later.

- Make sure that you haven't exceeded [your iCloud storage limit](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/mm039c13d410/) . If you exceed your iCloud storage limit, learn how to [make more space available in iCloud](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108922#make_space) .

- Make sure that you haven't exceeded any [mailbox size and message sending limits in iCloud](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102198) . If you're trying to attach a large file, try using [Mail Drop](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/mm6b1a7a10/) or [compressing the file](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102532) before sending it. Or use [AirDrop](https://support.apple.com/en-us/119857) , if the recipient is nearby.

- If you have [VPN configured on your iPhone](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102281) , turn it off, then try again.

- To check if you can receive mail for your iCloud email on iCloud.com, go to [icloud.com/mail](https://www.icloud.com/mail) .

- Turn your device off and back on.

### Check your iCloud Mail Push settings

- Tap Settings, then tap Apps.

- Tap Mail, then select Mail Accounts (Accounts).

- Tap Fetch New Data, then turn on Push.

- Under Fetch, check that you selected Automatically. If you have multiple email accounts, tap your iCloud account and turn on Push. You can only use Push with one account at a time.

- To check if Push is working, send an email to your account.

- Turn your device off and back on.

- If you still can't send or receive mail, [turn off iCloud Mail](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102562#turn_on_iCloud_Mail) on your device. Then turn off Push in the Fetch New Data menu. Restart your device, then turn these settings back on.

## If you can't send or receive mail from the Mail app on your Mac

Check that you turned on iCloud Mail on your Mac and check that your device and iCloud Mail account settings are up to date. Or check for your issue.

### Turn on iCloud Mail on your Mac

- Open the Mail app, then choose Settings from the Mail menu.

- Click Accounts, then select your iCloud account in the sidebar.

- Under Account Information, make sure that the account is enabled and the status is online.

### Check your device and account settings

- Make sure that you're using the [latest version of macOS](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108382) .

- Make sure that you're connected to the Internet and [check the system status of iCloud Mail](https://www.apple.com/support/systemstatus/) . If iCloud Mail isn't available, try again later.

- Make sure that you haven't exceeded [your iCloud storage limit](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/mm039c13d410/) . If you exceed your iCloud storage limit, learn how to [make more space available in iCloud](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108922#make_space) .

- Make sure that you haven't exceeded any [mailbox size and message sending limits in iCloud](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102198) . If you're trying to attach a large file, try using [Mail Drop](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/mm6b1a7a10/) or [compressing the file](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/mchlp2528/mac) before sending it. Or use [AirDrop](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/mh35868) , if the recipient is nearby.

- If you have [VPN configured on your Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102281) , turn it off, then try again.

- To check if you can receive mail for your iCloud email on iCloud.com, go to [icloud.com/mail](https://www.icloud.com/mail) .

- Turn your Mac off and back on.

### If you receive an alert when you send a message from your @icloud.com email address using Mail on your Mac

If you see an alert that says “This message could not be sent. It will remain in your Outbox until it can be sent.", make sure that iCloud is your Outgoing Mail Account.

- Open Mail. From the Mail menu, choose Settings.

- Click the Accounts tab.

- Select your iCloud email account.

- Click the Server Settings tab. If iCloud isn't listed as the Outgoing Mail Account, select iCloud. If you choose Edit SMTP Server List, you won't see your iCloud account listed as an option.

### If you get an “unknown address” or “undelivered mail returned” message in your Inbox

- Go to your Sent mailbox and make sure that the recipient's email address is correct.

- Check your Drafts, Sent, and Outbox mailboxes:

- If you see an unsent copy of the message in Drafts, try to send it again.

- If you see the message in your Outbox, make sure that you're connected to the Internet and that you [selected iCloud as the outgoing mail account](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102562#outgoing_mail_account) .*

- Ask the recipient to check their Junk mail folders or mailboxes.

- If your recipient still doesn't receive your message, but your message was sent without any alerts or “undelivered mail” messages, your message might be blocked or filtered by the iCloud Mail servers or the recipient's mail server. You can ask your recipient to contact their email administrator or contact Apple Support for help.

* If you use a non-iCloud SMTP server, don't use your iCloud email address as a "Send From" address. Otherwise, your email might not get delivered or it might get sent to the recipient's Junk folder.

### If you can receive messages but can't send them

Your ISP might be blocking the protocol used to send email (SMTP) across their network. Contact your service provider and ask about SMTP traffic that crosses their network to an external service, such as iCloud. If your service provider blocks SMTP traffic, ask them how they can exempt you from SMTP blocking.

## If you can't access iCloud Mail at iCloud.com

- Make sure that you're connected to the Internet and [check the system status of iCloud Mail](https://www.apple.com/support/systemstatus/) . If iCloud Mail isn't available, try again later.

- Make sure your browser is up to date. If you're using Safari, [update Safari to its latest version](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102665) .

- Clear your browser's cache.

- On your Mac, open Safari then choose Settings from the Safari menu. Click the Advanced tab. Select "Show features for web developers", then close Settings. From the Develop menu, choose "Empty Caches."

- On your iPhone, iPad, or Apple Vision Pro, go to Settings, tap Apps, then tap Safari. Scroll down and tap Clear History and Website Data. Confirm the timeframe that you want to clear, then tap Clear History.

- If you're using a different browser, check its help menu for steps.

- If you can access [iCloud Mail](https://www.icloud.com/mail) from a different location or Internet connection, contact your Internet service provider (ISP) or network administrator. Some Internet configurations intentionally or unintentionally prevent access to certain websites or services, such as [iCloud Mail](https://www.icloud.com/mail) .
