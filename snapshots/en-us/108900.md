# How to revive or restore Mac firmware

Rarely, a Mac with Apple silicon or the Apple T2 Security Chip might experience an issue that requires its firmware to be revived or restored by another Mac.

## When to revive or restore

If the firmware stored in its memory needs to be revived or restored, a Mac with Apple silicon or the Apple T2 Security Chip might experience issues such as the following. These can occur in certain rare circumstances, such as after a power failure interrupts macOS installation.

- Starts up to an [exclamation point in a circle](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102164)

- Turns on, but starts up to a blank screen that is not resolved by other [solutions for a blank screen](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102575)

- Shows the [status indicator light pattern](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102768) for firmware recovery mode

- Experiences a macOS installation issue not resolved by other [solutions for macOS installation errors](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102531)

## What you need to revive or restore

- The affected Mac, which is the [Mac with Apple silicon](https://support.apple.com/en-us/116943) or [Mac with the Apple T2 Security Chip](https://support.apple.com/en-us/103265) that you're reviving or restoring. Other Mac models don't apply.

- Another Mac of any model using [macOS 14 or later](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109033) . You will use this host Mac to revive or restore the affected Mac. It should be connected to the internet* and have enough available storage space to download the data it needs to revive or restore (32GB should be more than enough).

- A USB-C to USB-C cable that supports data and charging, such as the Apple USB-C Charge Cable included with some Apple products. It works with [ports on Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109523) that accept a type USB-C connector. Don't use a Thunderbolt 3 cable.

## How to prepare a laptop to revive or restore

You will use the USB-C cable to connect the two Mac computers, then enter DFU (device firmware update) mode on the affected Mac.

- Make sure that both Mac computers are connected to power.

- On the affected Mac laptop:

- Plug the USB-C cable into the DFU port. [Learn how to identify the DFU port](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120694) .

- Make sure that nothing is plugged into any other USB port, except as needed to connect your Mac to power. If your Mac has a MagSafe port, use that port for power instead of a USB port.

- On the host Mac:

- Plug the other end of the USB-C cable into any USB-C port.

- Make sure that this Mac is turned on and connected to the internet.*

- On the affected Mac laptop, enter DFU mode:

- Press and hold the power (Touch ID) button for up to 10 seconds, until the Mac turns off. If the Mac turns on instead, repeat this step.

- Press and release the power button, then immediately press and hold all four of these together on the built-in keyboard:

- Control ⌃ on the left side of the keyboard

- Option ⌥ on the left side of the keyboard

- Shift ⇧ on the right side of the keyboard

- Power button

[Image: MacBook Pro keyboard with highlighted keys needed for entering DFU mode to revive or restore firmware.]

- Mac laptop with Apple silicon:

- Keep holding all four keys for about 10 seconds, then release all keys—except the power button.

- Keep holding the power button for up to 10 more seconds, until the host Mac shows a [DFU window in the Finder](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108900#reviveorrestore) . If it first shows an alert asking you to allow the accessory to connect, release the power button and click Allow.

- Mac laptop with T2 chip: Keep holding all four keys for about 3 seconds, until the host Mac shows a [DFU window in the Finder](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108900#reviveorrestore) . If it first shows an alert asking you to allow the accessory to connect, release all keys and click Allow.

- The affected Mac is now in DFU mode and should show a blank screen. Follow the [steps to revive or restore](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108900#reviveorrestore) .

## How to prepare a desktop computer to revive or restore

You will use the USB-C cable to connect the two Mac computers, then enter DFU (device firmware update) mode on the affected Mac.

- On the affected Mac:

- Plug the USB-C cable into the DFU port. [Learn how to identify the DFU port](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120694) .

- Make sure that nothing is plugged into any other USB port.

- On the host Mac:

- Plug the other end of the USB-C cable into any USB-C port.

- Make sure that this Mac is turned on and connected to power and the internet.*

- On the affected Mac, enter DFU mode:

- Unplug the Mac from power.

- Press and hold the power button.

- Keep holding the power button while plugging the Mac into power, and continue holding it for up to 10 seconds, until the host Mac shows a [DFU window in the Finder](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108900#reviveorrestore) . If it first shows an alert asking you to allow the accessory to connect, release the power button and click Allow.

- The affected Mac is now in DFU mode and should show a blank screen. Follow the steps to revive or restore.

## How to revive or restore

After preparing your computers to revive or restore, the Finder on the host Mac should show a DFU window. The DFU window shows "Mac" selected in the sidebar and "Mac DFU Mode" on the right. [Don't see the DFU window?](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108900#help)

[Image: Finder window showing "Mac" selected in the sidebar.]

### Revive

Try reviving first. It can be faster than restoring, and it doesn't erase your Mac.

- From the DFU window in the Finder, click Revive Mac. Then click Continue to confirm.

- If this Mac is using an earlier version of macOS than the Mac being revived, you might be asked to update the software on this Mac before you can continue.

- A progress bar in this window shows that the revive is underway. Make sure that this Mac remains awake until the revive is complete.

- After the revive is complete, the affected Mac restarts automatically. If it shuts down instead, press the power button to turn it on.

- If asked, select the volume to recover (such as Macintosh HD), then click Next.

- If asked, select a user you know the password for, then enter that user's login password. Click Next, then click Restart.

- If reviving a Mac with Apple silicon, the revived Mac might [load startup options](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102342) , which shows your startup disk (such as Macintosh HD) and Options with a gear icon. Select your startup disk, then click the Continue button that appears below it.

- The revived Mac finishes starting up and the process is complete.

### Restore

Try reviving your Mac first. If your Mac can't be revived, follow these steps to erase it and restore it to factory settings.

- From the DFU window in the Finder, click Restore Mac. Then click Restore and Update to confirm.

- If this Mac is using an earlier version of macOS than the Mac being restored, you might be asked to update the software on this Mac before you can continue.

- A progress bar in this window shows that the restore is underway. Make sure that this Mac remains awake until the restore is complete.

- After the restore is complete, the affected Mac restarts automatically. If it shuts down instead, press the power button to turn it on.

- If asked, choose a Wi-Fi network or attach a network cable.

- Mac with Apple silicon:

- If asked, sign in to the Apple Account previously used with this restored Mac.

- When the setup assistant opens, use it to finish setting up your Mac.

- Mac with T2 chip:

- The restored Mac shows a spinning globe as it starts up from macOS Recovery over the internet. Choose your language when asked.

- If asked, sign in to the Apple Account previously used with this restored Mac. Then click Exit to Recovery.

- When you see the list of utilities in Recovery, choose the option to [install macOS](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102655#reinstall) .

- After macOS installation, the Mac restarts to a setup assistant. Use it to finish setting up your Mac.

## If you don't see a DFU window in the Finder

After preparing your computers to revive or restore, if you don’t see a DFU window in the Finder:

- From the Finder menu in the menu bar, choose Settings. Click Sidebar at the top of the settings window, then make sure that that "CDs, DVDs, and iOS Devices" is selected.

- Look for "Mac" in the Locations section of the sidebar of any Finder window. Select it, if present.

- Disconnect the USB-C cable from the affected Mac, then press and hold the power button for up to 10 seconds to turn off the affected Mac. Reconnect the USB-C cable to the [DFU port](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120694) , make sure that nothing is plugged into any other USB port, then try again to enter DFU mode. Proper timing of key presses is important.

- Try a different USB-C to USB-C cable. It must support both data and charging, and it should not be a Thunderbolt 3 cable. Plug the cable directly into each Mac, not into a USB hub or other device.
