# If your Mac doesn't start up all the way

If your Mac doesn't start up all the way or needs you to do something during startup, it might stop at one of these screens.

## Question mark

[Image: macOS startup screen displaying a question mark folder icon and URL: support.apple.com/mac/startup]

Learn what to do [if your Mac starts up to a question mark](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102601) .

## Circle with a line through it

[Image: macOS startup screen displaying a prohibitory sign and URL: support.apple.com/mac/startup]

Learn what to do [if your Mac starts up to a circle with a line through it](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101666) (prohibitory symbol).

## Globe with warning symbol

[Image: macOS startup screen displaying globe with alert symbol and the URL: support.apple.com/mac/startup]

A globe with a warning symbol (exclamation point) means that your Mac tried to start up from macOS Recovery over the internet, but couldn't. Learn what to do [if you can't start up from macOS Recovery](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102518) .

## Apple logo

[Image: Apple logo startup screen with progress bar]

Learn what to do [if your Mac starts up to an Apple logo or progress bar](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102535) .

## Blank (empty) screen

[Image: Blank gray screen]

Learn what to do [if your Mac starts up to a blank screen](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102575) , such as an empty gray, black, or blue screen.

## Options with a gear icon

[Image: macOS startup options screen showing Macintosh HD and Options icons]

Learn what to do [if your Mac starts up to Options with a gear icon](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102342) , also known as startup options.

## Exclamation point in a circle

[Image: Exclamation point in a circle]

Learn what to do [if your Mac starts up to an exclamation point in a circle](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102164) .

## Recovery Assistant

[Image: Recovery Assistant in macOS Tahoe 26.]

Learn what to do [if your Mac starts up to Recovery Assistant](https://support.apple.com/en-us/123922) , with a first-aid symbol (cross in a circle) and the message that your Mac encountered an issue while starting.

## Lock icon

[Image: macOS startup screen displaying firmware lock icon and password entry field]

If your Mac is using a [firmware password](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102384) , the lock icon appears when you try to start up from another disk or volume, such as an external drive or macOS Recovery. Enter the firmware password to continue.

## Login window

[Image: macOS Sonoma user login screen]

At the login window, enter your user account password to log in to your Mac. If FileVault is turned on, this also unlocks your disk. You might see a default desktop picture in the background, which might change to your chosen desktop picture when you select your account. The login window appears only after you log out of your user account (Apple menu  > Log Out) or restart or start up your Mac. Learn what to do [if you forgot your login password](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102633) .

If you're currently logged in to your user account, you might see your [lock screen](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/lock-the-screen-of-your-mac-mchl8e8b6a34/mac) instead. Your lock screen looks similar and uses the same login password, but doesn't provide password-reset options.

## Activation Lock or system lock PIN code

- If your previously owned Mac shows an Activation Lock window that asks for someone else's Apple Account, the previous owner may need to remove Activation Lock for you. [Learn about Activation Lock on Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102541) .

- Your Mac might ask for a PIN code when it has been remotely locked using Find My because the owner thinks the [Mac is lost or stolen](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102481) . Enter the four-digit or six-digit passcode to continue.

## Image of mouse or keyboard

If you see a line drawing of the underside of a Magic Mouse or backside of a Magic Keyboard, with a small arrow next to the power switch in the drawing, your Mac can’t detect your built-in or external mouse, trackpad, or keyboard. Learn what to do [if your keyboard or mouse won’t connect during Mac setup](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108797) .

## Display icon with warning symbol

A large display icon with a warning symbol against a dark background on your Apple Studio Display means that your [Studio Display is in recovery mode](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118251) .

## Thunderbolt, USB, or FireWire symbol

A large Thunderbolt , USB , or FireWire symbol against a dark background means that your Mac is in [target disk mode](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/transfer-files-mac-computers-target-disk-mode-mchlp1443/mac) .
