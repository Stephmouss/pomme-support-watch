# About Connectivity Assist

Use cellular data in addition to Wi-Fi for a more reliable internet connection.

Connectivity Assist uses cellular data to improve your connection when Wi-Fi is slow or unusable — in places like your backyard or driveway, or at coffee shops, airports, sports venues, and everywhere in between.

Connectivity Assist appears in Wi-Fi settings — this replaces and supersedes Wi-Fi Assist, previously found in Cellular settings. If Wi-Fi Assist was on when you updated your software, Connectivity Assist is on by default. If Wi-Fi Assist was off, you can turn Connectivity Assist on manually.

## Turn Connectivity Assist on or off

- [Update your iPhone to the latest iOS or iPadOS](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118575) .

- In Settings, tap Wi-Fi, then make sure Wi-Fi is turned on.

- Scroll down and turn Connectivity Assist on or off. If you want to always stay on Wi-Fi for a specific router configuration (for example, your ad blocker), you can [turn off Connectivity Assist for that particular network](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/manage-wi-fi-settings-iphw5gjwl8k2/ios#iphac9e3fcd6) .

With Connectivity Assist turned on, you might use more cellular data. If you want, you can [check or reset your cellular data usage](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109323) .

Connectivity Assist isn't used when your iPhone is international roaming, in cellular Low Data Mode, or with some third-party apps that stream audio and video.
