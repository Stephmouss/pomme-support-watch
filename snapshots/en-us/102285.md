# Use Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 networks with Apple devices

Apple devices that can join a Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 network can use the network’s 6 GHz Wi-Fi band to enable faster and more reliable Wi-Fi connections. Or they can be configured to use only the 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz bands of that network.

## What you need to join a Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 network

To join a Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 network, you need an Apple device that supports Wi-Fi 6E or later:

- [MacBook Pro](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108052) introduced in 2023 or later

- [MacBook Air](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102869) introduced in 2024 or later

- MacBook Neo

- [iMac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108054) introduced in 2023 or later

- [Mac mini](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102852) introduced in 2023 or later

- [Mac Studio](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102231) introduced in 2023 or later

- [Mac Pro](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102887) introduced in 2023 or later

- [iPhone](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108044) 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max, or later (excluding iPhone 15, iPhone 15 Plus, iPhone 16e, iPhone 17e)

- [iPad Pro](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108043#ipadpro) 11-inch (4th generation), iPad Pro 12.9 inch (6th generation), or later

- [iPad Air](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108043#ipadair) 11-inch (M2), iPad Air 13-inch (M2), or later

- [iPad mini](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108043#ipadmini) (A17 Pro) or later

To create a Wi-Fi 6E network, you need a Wi-Fi router or access point that supports Wi-Fi 6E. To create a Wi-Fi 7 network, you need a Wi-Fi router or access point that supports Wi-Fi 7.

## About Wi-Fi 6E and Wi-Fi 7 networks that have limited compatibility

For best performance with Apple devices, the Wi-Fi router providing the network should use a single network name (SSID) across all of its Wi-Fi bands: the 2.4 GHz band, the 5 GHz band, and the 6 GHz band.

If the router isn’t using the same name for all three bands, your device identifies the network as having limited compatibility. It then finds the name of the corresponding 5 GHz network and asks whether you want to join it for better compatibility:

[Image: iOS dialog asking whether to also join Wi-Fi 5G]

- If you select OK or Join, your device joins the 5 GHz network without some benefits of Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7.

- If you select Not Now, your device continues to use the 6 GHz network, but your overall experience with some activities over the network might not be as expected.

If you administer the Wi-Fi router, you can use its configuration webpage or app to edit the name of each band so that all bands have the same name. Your device can then rejoin the 6 GHz network with full compatibility. For more information about network names and other settings, consult the [recommended settings for Wi-Fi routers and access points](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102766) .

## How to turn off Wi-Fi 6E mode on your device

For Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 networks that use a single network name (SSID) as recommended, if you experience any issues with your use of the network, you can turn off Wi-Fi 6E mode when connected to that network. Your device will then no longer use that network’s 6 GHz band. Use of Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 on other networks isn't affected.

### Mac

- From the Apple menu , choose System Settings.

- Click Wi-Fi, then click the Details button next to the name of the Wi-Fi 6E network you're connected to.

- From the pop-up menu next to Wi-Fi 6E mode, choose Off. This option appears only when your [supported device](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102285#requirements) is connected to a Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 network.

### iPhone, iPad

- Open Settings, then tap Wi-Fi.

- Tap the name of the Wi-Fi 6E network you’re connected to.

- Tap Wi-Fi 6E mode, then tap Off. This option appears only when your [supported device](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102285#requirements) is connected to a Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 network.
