# About Communication Safety on your child's Apple device

If your child receives or attempts to send photos or videos that might contain sensitive content — including nudity or gore and violence — Communication Safety warns them, blurs the content, and provides helpful resources.

## Check for sensitive photos and videos

Communication Safety helps protect your child from viewing or sharing photos or videos that contain sensitive content, including nudity or gore and violence.

- If Communication Safety detects that your child receives this type of sensitive content, it blurs the photo or video before your child can view it.

- Communication Safety presents multiple interventions before your child can view or send sensitive content.

- With these interventions, Communication Safety provides guidance and age-appropriate resources to help them make a safe choice, including the choice to contact someone that they trust.

- When you set a Screen Time passcode, children under 13* can't view the sensitive content without permission. To view the content, they need to have a parent or guardian enter the Screen Time passcode.

* [Age requirements vary by state, country, or region](https://support.apple.com/en-us/125666)

### How Communication Safety protects your child's privacy

Communication Safety uses on-device machine learning to determine if a photo or video appears to contain sensitive content, including nudity or gore and violence. Because the photos and videos are analyzed on your child's device, Apple doesn't receive an indication that sensitive content was detected and doesn't get access to the photos or videos.

### Where Communication Safety detects sensitive photos and videos

Communication Safety detects sensitive content in these apps and services:

- On iPhone or iPad: Messages, AirDrop, Contact Posters in the Phone app and the Contacts app, FaceTime calls and video messages*, shared photo albums, and when your child selects a photo or video to share in some third-party apps.

- On Mac: Messages, Contact Posters in the Phone app and the Contacts app, shared photo albums, and when your child selects a photo or video to share in some third-party apps.

- On Apple Watch: Messages, Contact Posters in the Phone app and the Contacts app, and FaceTime video messages.

- On Apple Vision Pro*: Messages, AirDrop, and when your child selects a photo or video to share in some third-party apps.

Gore and violence detection is available only in FaceTime video messages, not live calls. It isn't available on Apple Vision Pro.

## Turn Communication Safety on or off in Screen Time settings

- Open Screen Time settings:

- iPhone, iPad, or Apple Vision Pro: Go to Settings, then tap Screen Time.

- Mac: Choose the Apple menu , click System Settings, then click Screen Time.

- Scroll down and select your child.

- Select Communication Safety.

- Turn Communication Safety on or off. You might need to enter the Screen Time passcode for the device.

### Communication Safety requirements

Manage Communication Safety settings for your child's device:

- Apple Account: Your child must be signed in to their device with a Child Account.

- Family Sharing: Your child must be part of your Family Sharing group.

Software requirements:

Your Apple device and your child's Apple device need supported software versions.

- For a complete set of protections: Update all of the devices in your Family Sharing group to the latest software version. This also makes sure that your Communication Safety settings sync across devices.

- Communication Safety in Messages: iOS 15.2 or later, iPadOS 15.2 or later, watchOS 9 or later, macOS Monterey 12.1 or later, and visionOS 2 or later

- Communication Safety in other supported apps: iOS 17 or later, iPadOS 17 or later, watchOS 10 or later, macOS Sonoma or later, and visionOS 2 or later

- Gore and violence detection: iOS 27 or later, iPadOS 27 or later, macOS 27 Golden Gate or later, and watchOS 27 or later

Default settings:

- Children under 18*: On by default on iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Apple Watch with the latest software version. For children 13-17, Communication Safety is turned on by default on Apple Vision Pro with the latest software version.

Apple Vision Pro is designed to fit and be used by individuals 13 years of age and older.

* [Age requirements vary by state, country, or region](https://support.apple.com/en-us/125666)

## What your child sees when sensitive content is detected

If Communication Safety determines that a photo or video your child has received or is about to send appears to contain sensitive content — including nudity or gore and violence — it blurs the photo or video and displays a warning.

### Ways to get help

Communication Safety offers your child several ways to get help. They can message a trusted adult, leave the conversation or group message, block the contact, and access online safety resources. It also reassures your child that it’s okay if they don’t want to view or send the photo or video, or if they want to leave the conversation.

If nudity is detected in an iMessage or FaceTime video call, your child can also report the sender to Apple. When they do, the sensitive content is sent to Apple for review.

Apple may forward reports to the relevant authorities, including law enforcement, and take appropriate action on the sender's Apple Account. Additionally, Apple may follow up regarding the report. This follow-up will be sent to the submitter of the report.

### Message someone they trust

As an additional precaution, your child has the option to message an adult that they trust about the photo or video. For children under 13*, Communication Safety prompts your child to start a conversation with their parent or guardian.

[Image: Communication Safety prompts your child to send a message to a trusted adult.]

* [Age requirements vary by state, country, or region](https://support.apple.com/en-us/125666)

### If your child chooses to view or send a photo or video that contains sensitive content

- Communication Safety confirms that they’re sure they want to do so and suggests alternatives, again reassuring your child that it’s okay not to participate and that more help is available.

- For children under 13* to view the sensitive content, they need to have their parent or guardian enter the Screen Time passcode on the child's device. This additional protection starts in iOS 18, iPadOS 18, macOS Sequoia, and visionOS 2.

- After the parent or guardian enters the Screen Time passcode, they can use a slider to selectively reveal details without viewing the full content. That way, full exposure to the sensitive content isn't required before making a decision about whether your child can view it. This feature starts in iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27 Golden Gate, watchOS 27, and visionOS 27.

## About Sensitive Content Warning

For adults, you can also turn on Sensitive Content Warning on your Apple devices to help you avoid receiving unwanted sensitive content, including nudity or gore and violence.
