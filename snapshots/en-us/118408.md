# Switch apps on your iPhone, iPad, or iPod touch

Learn how to quickly switch from one app to another. Then when you switch back, you can pick up right where you left off.

## Switch apps on iPhone X or later, and iPad

If you have an iPhone X or later, or an iPad:

- Swipe up from the bottom to the middle of your screen and hold until you see the App Switcher.

- Swipe left or right to find the app that you want to use.

- Tap the app.

[Image: To open the App Switcher, swipe up from the bottom to the middle of your iPhone or iPad screen.]

If you have a [Smart Keyboard](https://support.apple.com/108361) or [Bluetooth keyboard](https://support.apple.com/105108) paired to your iPad, press Command-Tab to switch between apps.

## Switch apps with the Home button

If you have an iPhone or an iPad with a Home button:

- Double-click the Home button to see recently used apps.

- Swipe left or right to find the app that you want to use.

- Tap the app.

## Use Background App Refresh

After you switch to a different app, some apps run for a short period of time before they're set to a suspended state. Apps that are in a suspended state aren’t actively in use, open, or taking up system resources. With Background App Refresh, suspended apps can check for updates and new content.

If you want suspended apps to check for new content, turn on Background App Refresh:

- In the Settings app, tap General.

- Tap Background App Refresh.

- Tap Background App Refresh once more then turn the feature off or select whether to use Background App Refresh with Wi-Fi or with Wi-Fi & Cellular Data.

[Image: You can choose to use Background App Refresh on Wi-Fi or on Wi-Fi or Cellular Data.]

If you quit an app from the app switcher, it might not be able to run or check for new content before you open it again.

If your [iPhone seems to run slowly](https://support.apple.com/102598) or its [battery-life drains too quickly](https://support.apple.com/120745) , closing all of your apps does not impact your device's overall performance. Recently used apps only use resources and power when they're active or when they're performing specific background tasks or services.
