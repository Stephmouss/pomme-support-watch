# Set a firmware password on your Mac

When you set a firmware password on your Intel-based Mac, users who don't have the password can't start up from macOS Recovery or any disk other than the designated startup disk.

This feature requires a Mac with an Intel processor. For the equivalent level of security on a [Mac with Apple silicon](https://support.apple.com/en-us/116943) , simply [turn on FileVault](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/encrypt-mac-data-with-filevault-mh11785/mac) . If the Mac is managed by MDM (mobile device management), the MDM administrator can also [remotely lock](https://support.apple.com/guide/deployment/lock-and-locate-devices-depb980a0be4/web) the Mac.

- [Turn on a firmware password](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102384#turnon)

- [Turn off a firmware password](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102384#turnoff)

- [If you forgot the firmware password](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102384#forgot)

## How to turn on a firmware password

This information does not apply to Mac computers with Apple silicon. If you have an Intel-based Mac, follow these steps:

- [Start up from macOS Recovery](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102518) . Then return to this article and continue to the next step.

- From the Utilities menu at the top of the screen in Recovery, choose Startup Security Utility (or Firmware Password Utility).

- Enter your administrator password when prompted. This is the password you use to log in to your Mac.

- Click Turn On Firmware Password, then enter a firmware password. [Remember this password](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102384#forgot) !

- Quit the utility, then choose Restart from the Apple menu .

When using a firmware password, your Mac requires the firmware password in these circumstances:

- When attempting to start up from a storage device other than the one [selected in Startup Disk settings](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/change-your-mac-startup-disk-mchlp1034/mac) .

- When attempting to start up from macOS Recovery.

- When attempting to use the [Erase All Content and Settings feature](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102664) (Mac with Apple T2 Security Chip).

## How to turn off a firmware password

This information does not apply to Mac computers with Apple silicon. If you have an Intel-based Mac, follow these steps:

- [Start up from macOS Recovery](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102518) . Before your Mac can finish starting up from Recovery, it will show the lock screen pictured above. Enter the firmware password. [Forgot the password?](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102384#forgot)

- After your Mac finishes starting up from Recovery, choose Startup Security Utility (or Firmware Password Utility) from Utilities menu at the top of the screen.

- Click Turn Off Firmware Password, then enter the firmware password again.

- Quit the utility, then choose Restart from the Apple menu .

## If you forgot the firmware password

If you can't remember your firmware password, [get your Mac ready for service](https://support.apple.com/en-us/116942) and schedule an in-person service appointment with an Apple Store or Apple Authorized Service Provider. Bring your Mac and its original receipt or invoice as proof of purchase.
