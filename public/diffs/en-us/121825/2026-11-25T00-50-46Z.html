37#### Magnifier
37#### Magnifier
38
38
39Magnifier works like a digital magnifying glass to detect, zoom in on, and describe physical objects. Learners can format text within the frame for easier reading, read textbook or whiteboard words aloud, or use Apple Intelligence for detailed visual descriptions, answers to follow-up questions, and voice controls like "zoom in" or "turn on flashlight." Detection Mode uses on-device machine learning to scan surroundings, with LiDAR-equipped iPads providing spatial feedback for detecting nearby people, doors, and furniture. 5 On Mac, Magnifier connects to an external camera to zoom in on surroundings—such as a screen, a whiteboard, or a printed worksheet—while multiple live session windows let students multitask between viewing a presentation and a book using Desk View. These views can be captured, grouped, and saved to return to later.

43#### Braille Access
44
44
45Braille Access is a braille-first experience that turns iPad or Mac into a fully integrated braille notetaker. With a built-in app launcher, students can open apps via connected braille devices, access Braille Ready Format (BRF) files, take notes in braille format, and perform calculations using Nemeth Braille or UEB Math, two codes often used in classrooms for math and science. Integrated Live Captions enable students and teachers to transcribe conversations in real time on braille displays, while Apple Intelligence generates summaries to help students who read braille at a slower pace follow along without missing the thread of a lesson or conversation. 3

55#### Display settings & Text Size
56
56
57Display settings and text size tools enlarge content and optimize contrast so students with low vision can read textbooks, notes, and assignments without eye strain. Students can adjust text size with Larger Text or add weight with Bold Text. They can also invert colors for a dark background that helps text stand out, add Color Filters for vision deficiencies, tone down bright displays with Reduce White Point, enhance foreground-background contrast with Increase Contrast, or make backgrounds solid using Reduce Transparency. These settings can be assigned globally or per-app, ensuring a student's preferences follow them across every subject.

167#### AssistiveTouch

168
169AssistiveTouch helps students adapt their touchscreens to fit their physical needs. If certain gestures—like a pinch or a two-finger tap—don't work, a student can swap them with a gesture that does or create a touch that is all their own. Students can customize the layout of the AssistiveTouch menu or connect a Bluetooth device to control an on-screen pointer, tailoring the pointer experience by adjusting its color, shape, size, scrolling speed, and more to make navigation easier and more precise. They can also use dwell, sound actions, or custom gestures to control their device in the way that works best for them. With the Virtual Trackpad on iPad, students and teachers can control their device using a small region of the screen as a resizable trackpad.

173#### Touch Accommodations

175If a screen responds to unintended actions, or repeated or misplaced taps, students can customize how their device reacts to touches, taps, and swipes using Touch Accommodations. It's quicker than ever to set up and configure Touch Accommodations to prevent accidental selections with Hold Duration, which adjusts how long a touch needs to be made before the screen responds. If tremors or limited dexterity cause a single tap to register multiple times, Ignore Repeat can recognize only one touch within a specific timeframe while ignoring the rest. With Tap Assistance students can adjust their finger placement before a tap is officially recognized.
