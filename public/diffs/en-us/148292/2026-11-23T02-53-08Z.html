5## About virtual productions and synchronization
6
6
7In a virtual production, live performances are filmed in front of a dynamic, synchronized video background. For a successful virtual production, the video playback device and the cameras must share the same timing reference. When the devices are properly synchronized, each frame of your recording is perfectly in sync and helps you achieve professional, frame-accurate edits without hours of manual frame-by-frame alignment.

175If the genlock signal doesn't arrive at your Mac and iPhone devices at the same time, the background video might be delayed slightly. A variety of factors can contribute to this delay, including the use of physical video adapters and certain types of advanced processing on the display itself. If the delay is significant enough, it can create a faint doubling of fast moving images (also called ghosting artifacts) in the video background on the recorded video.

1993. Camera sensors in compatible iPhone models use a technique called “rolling shutter” to read out the image data. This means that not all of the pixels from the camera sensor are captured at the same time. Additionally, the direction in which the pixels are captured is fixed. Similarly, the pixels of an LED or OLED display (or for that matter, any raster display) are not all illuminated at the same time. The direction in which the display’s pixels are illuminated is also fixed. For displays, the direction is left-to-right and top-to-bottom for the standard, horizontal orientation of the display. Similarly, for the iPhone sensors, the direction is left-to-right and top-to-bottom if the phone is held in a horizontal position with the cameras on the back at the top of the phone. For best results, both the display and the phone should have similar orientations such that their scan-out and read-out directions are the same.
